End matterNotation and Glossary

A quick reference for the symbols and terms used across the book. The last column links to the chapter where each is introduced.

Notation

\(k(x, x')\)A kernel: a symmetric similarity function between two examples. Positive definite kernels are the ones this book studies. Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
\(\mathbf{K},\; K_{ij}=k(x_i,x_j)\)The Gram matrix, the table of pairwise kernel values on a sample. Positive definiteness of the kernel means every Gram matrix is positive semidefinite. Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
\(\Phi(x)\)The feature map sending an example into the feature space, with k(x,x') = \langle\Phi(x),\Phi(x')\rangle. Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
\(\mathcal{H}\)The reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) associated with a kernel: the space of functions in which the kernel acts as an inner product. Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
\(\langle f, g\rangle_{\mathcal H},\; \lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal H}\)The RKHS inner product and norm. The norm measures smoothness: a small norm means a slowly varying function. Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
\(k(\cdot, x) = K_x\)The representer of evaluation at x: the function whose RKHS inner product with any f returns f(x), the reproducing property. Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
\(\alpha,\; f=\sum_i \alpha_i k(\cdot, x_i)\)The dual coefficients of a kernel machine. The representer theorem guarantees the solution has this finite form. The Kernel Trick and the Representer Theorem
\(\lambda\)The regularization parameter weighting the RKHS-norm penalty against the data-fitting loss. Kernel Ridge Regression and Smooth Losses
\(\ell(y, f(x))\)A loss function scoring the fit of a prediction. Different losses (squared, hinge, logistic, epsilon-insensitive) give different algorithms. Kernel Ridge Regression and Smooth Losses
\(\xi_i,\; C,\; \nu\)SVM slack variables and the trade-off parameters: C prices margin violations, and nu bounds the fraction of margin errors and support vectors. Support Vector Machines
\(\lambda_i,\; \psi_i\)The eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the kernel integral operator (Mercer's theorem). Their decay measures the smoothness the kernel enforces. Mercer's Theorem, Spectra, and Rates
\(\mu_P = \mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}[k(\cdot, X)]\)The kernel mean embedding of a probability distribution P into the RKHS. Mean Embeddings, MMD, and Characteristic Kernels
\(\mathrm{MMD}(P, Q)\)The maximum mean discrepancy, the RKHS distance between the mean embeddings of P and Q. Mean Embeddings, MMD, and Characteristic Kernels

Glossary

Bochner's theoremA continuous translation-invariant kernel is the Fourier transform of a nonnegative measure; the basis of random Fourier features. Translation-Invariant, Semigroup, and Probabilistic Kernels
Characteristic kernelA kernel whose mean embedding is injective, so MMD(P,Q)=0 only when P=Q; guaranteed by universality. Mean Embeddings, MMD, and Characteristic Kernels
Conditionally positive definite kernelA kernel whose quadratic form is nonnegative on zero-sum coefficient vectors; k is CPD iff exp(tk) is positive definite for all t>0. Translation-Invariant, Semigroup, and Probabilistic Kernels
Convolution (R-)kernelHaussler's framework building a kernel on structured objects by summing a kernel over all ways of decomposing them into parts; the umbrella over string, tree, and graph kernels. Kernels for and on Graphs
Kernel PCAPrincipal component analysis run through the kernel: the eigendecomposition of the centered Gram matrix. Kernel PCA, Clustering, and CCA
Kernel ridge regressionLeast-squares regression in an RKHS with a norm penalty; its solution is a linear system over the Gram matrix. Kernel Ridge Regression and Smooth Losses
Kernel trickReplacing every inner product in an algorithm by a kernel evaluation, so the algorithm runs in a feature space it never builds. The Kernel Trick and the Representer Theorem
Maximum mean discrepancy (MMD)The RKHS distance between two distributions' mean embeddings, estimable from samples and used for two-sample testing. Mean Embeddings, MMD, and Characteristic Kernels
Mercer's theoremA continuous positive definite kernel on a compact domain expands as a uniformly convergent sum of its eigenfunctions, giving an explicit feature map. Mercer's Theorem, Spectra, and Rates
Multiple kernel learningLearning a convex combination of several kernels jointly with the predictor, a group-lasso over kernels. Multiple Kernel Learning
Neural tangent kernel (NTK)The kernel governing an infinitely wide network's training in the lazy regime; one way deep networks connect to kernel machines. Deep Learning from a Kernel Point of View
Nyström approximationA low-rank approximation of the Gram matrix from a subset of columns, one of the two main routes past the quadratic-memory wall. Large-Scale Kernel Machines
Positive definite kernelA symmetric function whose Gram matrix is positive semidefinite for every finite sample; equivalently, an inner product after a feature map. Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
Rademacher complexityA data-dependent capacity measure: how well a function class can correlate with random noise. Controls generalization for RKHS balls. Learning Theory in RKHS Balls
Random Fourier featuresA finite random feature map whose inner products approximate a translation-invariant kernel (via Bochner), reducing the kernel machine to a linear model. Large-Scale Kernel Machines
Representer theoremThe minimizer of a regularized empirical risk over an RKHS lies in the span of the kernel functions at the training points, collapsing an infinite search to n coefficients. The Kernel Trick and the Representer Theorem
Reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)A Hilbert space of functions in which evaluation is continuous and is carried out by an inner product with the kernel. Every positive definite kernel has a unique RKHS (Aronszajn). Positive Definite Kernels and RKHS
Support vectorA training point with nonzero dual coefficient; the only points that determine an SVM's decision boundary. Support Vector Machines
Support vector machineA margin-maximizing classifier: the hinge loss plus an RKHS penalty, whose solution depends only on the support vectors. Support Vector Machines
